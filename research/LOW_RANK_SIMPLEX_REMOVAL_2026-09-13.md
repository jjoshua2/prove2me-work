# Low-rank simplex removal beyond the segment-free frontier

## 1. Contribution and boundary

This add-only continuation is based on PR #210 at
`4b82eedf97812059490e545a8a4d02822597f87b`. Earlier antipodal, segment-capacity,
LP, core and wall-lifting sources are unchanged. A separate agent handles
pinned Lean verification and platform submission.

The previous complete catalogue can prove that no segment is removable while
leaving a decomposable residual. The product of triangles was an explicit
example. This continuation provides:

1. An exact global maximal-summand test for an arbitrary supplied k-simplex,
   using linear elimination in k variables, not the ambient dimension d.
   Its complete obstruction list has supports of size at most k+1.
2. Automatic *local* triangle/simplex proposals from original vertex neighbors,
   followed by those global tests and actual original-H routes. These proposals
   are not a complete catalogue of higher-dimensional summands.
3. Exact counterexamples to two tempting extensions of the segment theory:
   one antipodal path need not contain a simplex factor's other edge directions,
   and maximal triangle removals need not commute.
4. A non-product, segment-free tetrahedron-plus-triangle example, and blind
   recovery of all triangle factors in high-dimensional affine products.

The principal extraction theorem is general; it is not conditioned on a supplied
diameter, vertex graph, coarse model, or an already-valid decomposition.
Minkowski summand recognition and Farkas elimination are classical subjects.
The contribution is the fixed-candidate-dimension certificate, its exact
original-H implementation, and its interaction with this research frontier.
No priority claim for every variant of these convex/LP identities is made.

The new Lean file is UNCOMPILED. It supplies exact lift equations and dual
obstruction cores, not a completed formal proof of the Farkas alternative or
positive-circuit generation. No new platform verdict or Polynomial Hirsch
conclusion is asserted.

## 2. Turn simplex subtraction into a k-variable feasibility problem

Let R={x in R^d : A_i x<=b_i}, with m original rows, be nonempty and compact.
The specified candidate is S=conv(0,g_1,...,g_k), with linearly independent
rational g_j. Translations of a candidate can be absorbed into the residual.
The code uses this origin-normalized representation.

Its exact original-row support values are

    h_i = max(0,A_i g_1,...,A_i g_k).

For t>=0 the erosion is

    E_t = {p : A_i p<=b_i-t h_i for every i}.

E_t+tS is always contained in R. Nonempty erosion alone does not prove equality,
just as it did not for segment extraction. To prove the reverse inclusion,
take an arbitrary x in R and ask for coefficients z in R^k satisfying

    z_j>=0,       sum_j z_j<=t,
    -(A_i G) z <= b_i-A_i x-t h_i.                         (1)

Here G has columns g_j. The last inequalities say that
p=x-sum_j z_j g_j belongs to E_t. Thus (1) is equivalent to x in E_t+tS.
The unknown dimension is k even when d is large.

Write C for the (m+k+1)-by-k matrix whose rows are -A_i G, followed by -e_j
and the all-ones row. Its right-hand side at x,t is

    r(x,t)=(b-Ax-t h, 0,...,0,t).

The classical finite Farkas alternative says that (1) is feasible exactly when

    w.r(x,t)>=0 for every w>=0 with w^T C=0.                (2)

These are algebraic elimination multipliers. The term "circuit" below means
a minimal linear dependence of these rows. It does NOT mean a circuit walk
or a replacement for ordinary polytope edges.

## 3. A complete small-support obstruction list

The cone K={w>=0 : w^T C=0} is pointed. Each extreme ray has a support I on
which the kernel is one-dimensional and spanned by a strictly positive vector.
Conversely such a vector is an extreme ray.

Indeed, if the restricted kernel has dimension at least two, perturb a positive
vector within that kernel in both directions by a sufficiently small amount;
it is not extreme. A ray with some zero coordinates should simply be considered
on its smaller support. Rank-nullity therefore gives

    |I|<=rank(C)+1=k+1.                                    (3)

The entire cone is generated by these rays. One elementary finite proof is
support reduction: a nonnegative kernel vector not on a ray has a nontrivial
kernel perturbation with both signs after subtracting a suitable multiple of
itself. Move in both directions until some coordinate vanishes. Express the
original as a convex combination of these smaller-support kernel vectors and
induct on support size. The base case is a one-dimensional positive kernel.
This proves the reduction without enumerating vertices of R.

The executable enumerates all row supports of sizes one through k+1, computes
their exact kernels and retains precisely the one-dimensional positive ones.
A zero C-row contributes its singleton ray only; it cannot be in any larger
minimal positive support. Skipping larger supports containing zero rows is
therefore a complete optimization, not an assumption that those original
inequalities can be discarded.

The independent verifier repeats this bounded-dimensional enumeration. A missing
or modified obstruction is rejected; it does not trust a claimed ray count or
assume that an observed subset is complete. A combinatorial search cap is checked
before enumeration. Exceeding it produces no completeness assertion.

For fixed k, there are O((m+k+1)^(k+1)) supports. In particular triangles give
a cubic support enumeration in m, independently of d. This is a polynomial-size
reduction to linear programming for fixed k, not a claim of a strongly polynomial
pivot rule. The exact Bland simplex used here can have exponential pivot behavior,
and k itself cannot grow without affecting the exponent.

## 4. The exact maximal scale and its proof certificates

Split a ray w into its original-row multipliers lambda_i, its multipliers for
-z_j<=0, and its final multiplier gamma>=0. Define

    c_w = sum_i lambda_i A_i,
    B_w = sum_i lambda_i b_i,
    D_w = sum_i lambda_i h_i-gamma.

Kernel annihilation implies sum_i lambda_i A_i g_j<=gamma for each j.
The pairing in (2) becomes

    w.r(x,t)=B_w-c_w x-t D_w.

If D_w<=0, this is automatically nonnegative for x in R and t>=0:
B_w-c_w x is a nonnegative combination of original row slacks.
Only rays with D_w>0 constrain removal. The maximal removable scale is exactly

    mu_S(R)=min_{w extreme, D_w>0}
                [B_w-max_{x in R} c_w x]/D_w.              (4)

For a nontrivial bounded R,S there is at least one such constraint and the
capacity is finite. The code explicitly declines an infinite-capacity case.
Each c_w is a nonnegative combination of original rows, so its support objective
is bounded above on R. Feasible primal and dual solutions are checked exactly.

For every positive-demand ray, the certificate gives NONNEGATIVE original-row
multipliers u_w satisfying

    u_w A = c_w,
    u_w b <= B_w-mu_S(R) D_w.                              (5)

Consequently every point of R satisfies (2) for the claimed scale, and Farkas
gives a decomposition of EVERY point. Combining this with erosion inclusion
proves the complete Minkowski equality R=E_mu+mu S.

A single feasible sharpness point x* and one ray w* attain equality in (4).
They prove no larger scale can work. This rules out a larger simplex with
ANY proposed residual, not just the erosion: if R=P+tS, then P is contained
in E_t, hence R is contained in E_t+tS and the same necessary inequality applies.

The checker validates every original normal identity and constant bound,
completeness of the positive-demand list, original feasibility of x*, and the
sharp equation. Verification does not run LP optimization. Rank-one candidates
are independently cross-checked with the preceding complete fiber-width formula.

### General low-dimensional candidate polytopes

The same mathematical argument is not specific to simplex facets. For a compact
candidate Q={Gz:Fz<=r} in k intrinsic coordinates, use auxiliary rows (-AG,F)
and right-hand side (b-Ax-t h,t r), where h_i=max_(q in Q) A_i q. The kernel
cone has extreme supports of size at most k+1. The identical formula uses

    D_w=lambda.h-nu.r

for w=(lambda,nu). Thus fixed-rank H-candidates have the same elimination
principle after certifying their support values and boundedness. The current
code implements independent-generator SIMPLEX candidates, for which supports
and the intrinsic facets require no additional optimization or supplied H/V
equivalence. General-candidate code is not claimed delivered.

## 5. Automatic proposals without pretending higher-dimensional completeness

The integrated entry point uses original A,b,start,end only, with a rank cap
(default two). It finds an independent active basis at the source. Releasing
one basis equality yields a direction; the first original blocking inequality
gives a feasible ordinary edge when the step is positive. At a simple source
this obtains all incident edges. At a nonsimple source it may obtain only some;
there is no completeness claim for that local selection.

Adjacent neighbor pairs suggest triangle shapes anchored at zero. These rank-two
proposals come from actual triangular two-faces, because the two source edges
retain d-2 independent common rows and their other endpoints are adjacent.
For rank three or higher, neighbor cliques merely SUGGEST simplex shapes; a
higher-dimensional clique is not asserted to be the entire corresponding face.
The global extractor, not the local proposal, is the only acceptance test.

Each candidate is tested against the CURRENT original-normal residual, and its
maximal amount is removed if positive. This sequential procedure is intentionally
order-sensitive. It is not a complete simplex catalogue or a canonical greatest
simplex factorization. Its purpose is to propose and certify nonsegment
decompositions on cases where the previous segment-free certificate stopped.

The residual is passed to the already implemented point/pyramid/feedback
recognizers. Original boundedness is separately certified by 2d Farkas coordinate
bounds; those are essential when using point or pyramid recognition. Endpoint
decompositions are determined by strict original exposing objectives. Every
output edge is rechecked against ALL original inequalities and their shared
active rank. There is no assumed graph-isomorphism or hidden diameter oracle.

When the residual is a point and the extracted simplex spans are independent,
the sum is an affine Cartesian product of simplices. A step changing one factor
vertex is a true original edge. Every path must change each differing factor,
and one edge changes only one factor. The route is therefore shortest, with
length equal to the number of differing simplex-factor choices. Independence
is verified by the rank of all concatenated generators, not guessed from a
nominal product label. Other decompositions use the unchanged ordinary-edge
wall lifter and receive its bound, not a shortestness claim.

## 6. Two segment arguments that DO NOT extend

### An antipodal walk does not expose every simplex direction

In T=conv(0,e1,e2), c=(2,1) uniquely maximizes at e1 and minimizes at 0.
The one-edge route e1--0 is antipodal, but sees only one of T's three directions.
The other two are missed although T itself is a simplex summand. The example
can be embedded as a proper summand of a product with another triangle.

Thus the earlier binary-allocation argument for segments cannot be reused
unchanged for a simplex with three or more vertices. Our local proposals are
explicitly not advertised as a complete replacement for the segment catalogue.

### Maximal triangle removals compete, even in a segment-free polygon

Let R be the pentagon with cyclic vertices

    (0,0),(3,0),(4,2),(2,4),(-1,2).

Consider the two origin-anchored triangles

    S=conv((0,0),(3,0),(9/2,3)),
    T=conv((0,0),(3,0),(9/5,6/5)).

The new exact certificates establish

    mu_S(R)=2/3,        mu_T(R)=1,
    mu_T(R minus (2/3)S)=0,
    mu_S(R minus T)=0.                                     (6)

The earlier complete segment catalogue independently certifies that R is
segment-free. The phenomenon is not caused by an unremoved segment.

This has an elementary edge-budget interpretation. The oriented edge vectors
of R are (3,0),(1,2),(-2,2),(-3,-2),(1,-2).
At scale2/3, S uses all of the (1,2) and (-3,-2) edge resources. T needs the
latter as well, so none of it can be removed afterward. At scale1, T consumes
all of the horizontal (3,0) edge, which S also needs. Simultaneously taking
both individual maxima would already require horizontal length 2+3>3.

Both orders give valid exact decompositions, but they are different.
Algebraic commutation of RHS subtraction at FIXED amounts does not imply
invariance of the MAXIMAL available amounts. This invalidates an order-free
"greatest collection of all simplex factors" extrapolation. It does not
invalidate the segment-specific commutation theorem.

The next selection issue is consequently a resource-allocation problem, not
just finding more candidate shapes. The present solver does not claim to
optimize that joint problem.

## 7. Actual segment-free examples and original-edge routes

The product of two triangles previously demonstrated a successful zero-segment
catalogue with an unsupported residual. The new automatic local proposals
recover both triangles, remove them with exact global certificates, identify
a point core, and route every pair with a matching shortestness lower bound.

A second example is NOT a direct product. Let

    P=conv(0,e1,e2,e3),
    Q=conv(0,(2,0,1),(0,3,1)).

The test independently forms the complete final H-description of R=P+Q.
R has dimension3, eight facets and nine vertices. Its complete segment
catalogue returns zero factors. From source(0,0,1), the new local proposal
recovers the triangle Q and its maximal scale1 without being supplied Q,
then recognizes the residual tetrahedron by the pyramid criterion.
All endpoint pairs are checked against the independently enumerated ORIGINAL
graph. The generic lifts are not all shortest.

Large successful final-H inputs:

| Dimension | Original facets | Recovered triangle factors | Vertices (formula) | Shortest test route |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|16|24|8|3^8=6,561|8|
|32|48|16|3^16=43,046,721|16|
|6, unknown dense affine chart|9|3|27|3|

No candidate shapes, lengths, factor chart or parent route are supplied.
The large graphs are not enumerated. These are deliberately explicit products,
not a claim of a new product-diameter theorem or new hard polytope family.
The achievement is automatic higher-dimensional factor recovery from final
inequalities after the segment criterion provides no factors. In the dense
case the chart, translation, row permutation and scaling are not supplied.

The exact extractor also handles a rank-three tetrahedral candidate, a
lower-dimensional embedded triangle, duplicated original facets and a constant
row. A square correctly has zero capacity for a candidate triangle. A
tetrahedron correctly has zero capacity for a proper rank-two triangle.

## 8. Independent checks and formalization

The test suite independently verifies whole Minkowski equality on seven small
models by fully enumerating the original and eroded H-vertices. Every original
extreme point must occur among sums of eroded vertices and simplex vertices.
Because every such sum is in R, this is an exact equality criterion, not a
sampling heuristic. Each capacity plus1/100 fails that independent equality
test. Forty-three explicit original-point decompositions are also checked.

Seven rank-one specializations agree with the older exact fiber extractor.
A separate Fourier--Motzkin implementation checks144 lifted feasibility
instances in candidate ranks1,2,3 against the positive-circuit test:
83 feasible and61 infeasible. It does not call the positive-circuit enumerator
to make its decision.

Three independent small original graphs have22 vertices,39 edges and178
ordered distances. All100 unordered endpoint pairs including stationary pairs
are routed, producing154 edge occurrences. Fifty-five have certified matching
lower bounds;20 other lifts are longer than the actual shortest distance.
Three larger/dense-affine routes bring the total to103 route certificates
and181 ordinary-edge occurrences. Twenty invalid or unsupported cases are
rejected, including omitted obstructions, invalid kernel multipliers,
oversized/undersized claimed maxima, false compactness, false shortestness,
a diagonal offered as an edge, and an unrecognized octahedral residual.

The new Lean module proves the exact erosion/lift equivalence, one-sided
erosion inclusion, the scalar dual obstruction, sharpness arithmetic, and
infeasibility from a negative nonnegative-kernel pairing. Its six printouts
have NOT been compiled/audited in this session. The full sufficiency of
Farkas, positive-circuit generation, model recognition and executable route
construction are distinct written/classical/executable dependencies.
A compiling finite obstruction core alone is not the end-to-end diameter
or decomposition theorem.

No previous file is changed. No workflow, dependency pin, platform child or
Prove2Me verdict is created. The current exact LP discovery has explicit caps
and no polynomial pivot guarantee. Universal simplex discovery, joint optimal
allocation of competing summands, and arbitrary segment-free residual routing
remain open tasks in this approach, not implied by the successful examples.
