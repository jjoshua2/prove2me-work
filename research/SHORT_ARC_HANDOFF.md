## New result

Every reference fallback endpoint maximizes the phase objective over every
inspected original two-face. Keep that EXACT endpoint and take a shortest
boundary arc to it, allowing intermediate objective decreases. No additional
faces or neighbors are searched. All decision/phase anchors, locked rows and
permanent retired-face labels are identical to #253's certified reference.

With e=m-d, r initially missing target facets and a=floor((e+2)/2), the written
bound becomes

    L <= a * (r + sum_{h=3}^r floor(binom(e,h-2)/(h-1))).

The earlier cost was e+1 per fallback. The count is still binomial/exponential;
there is no general polynomial route bound or best-known diameter improvement.
Intermediate repeats are handled by chronological loop erasure. Raw length
never increases relative to the reference; monotone domination of its separate
loop-erased output is NOT asserted.

## Two precise family statements

The clipped parabolic-prism family has N+h+8 genuine facets in dimension3.
#253 takes N+4 original edges; the same-anchor shortcut takes h+5, which is
shortest. At N64,h2 the counts are68 and7. The old long prefix is the fallback,
not an acquisition arc already shortened in #253. First phase has no visible
target facet in any incident two-face, so this is not a duplicate #252 polygon
lookahead example. The short arc includes h+1 decreasing edges and reaches the
same maximum. Fixed h gives an unbounded ratio of the raw route lengths.

The clipped-cube family in every dimension d has3d facets, and NO proper face
through the source meets ANY target facet. Yet first acquisition distance is d
and total source-target distance is exactly2d-1. Two-face rules need at least
ceil((d-2)/2) fallbacks before first acquisition; #253 attains that first-phase
count. These are all-dimensional written arguments, not extrapolated graph tests.
The family does not contradict Hirsch and does not preclude polynomial phases.
