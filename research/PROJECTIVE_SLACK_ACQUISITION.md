# Projective target-slack shares and endpoint-only face progress

## Status and actual contribution

Research continuation of #253 from main
`9456445941adbbfd6464d8bd1d559599d6f3ea18`. All previous selectors, verifiers,
accepted proofs, workflows and pins remain unchanged. The new input class is
still SIMPLE, bounded, full-dimensional ORIGINAL-H polytopes. This is written

The new policy is invariant under positive projective changes, not just affine
changes. Its fallback uses a shortest polygon arc and needs progress only at

bound. The potentially exponential number of retired faces remains unchanged.
Two exact counterexample families now exclude uniform fractional decrease of
BOTH the old radial gap and the new angular score at fixed dimension/facet count.

The benchmark is mixed, not hidden: on the same1,016 pairs, old2,506 edges versus
new2,517, with6 improved,13 worsened and997 tied pairs. The new code is a separate
research alternative, not a default replacement claiming universal superiority.

## 1. Score, endpoint conventions and projective invariance

Let P={x:A_i x<=b_i} have m genuine facets and dimension d. Let T be the d
independent rows tight at the target v. At a phase anchor p, lock
J=active(p) intersect T; put U=T minus J and h=|U|. The retained face P_J has
dimension h. Define, for x in that face,

    z_j(x)=(b_j-A_j x)/(b_j-A_j p), j in U,
    S(x)=sum_j z_j(x),
    r_j(x)=z_j(x)/S(x), rho(x)=min_j r_j(x).                 (1)

Before acquiring another target row all z_j are positive. S=0 only at v, by
independence of the target rows. At p, rho=1/h. At a new nonterminal target-facet
acquisition rho=0. The program assigns an all-zero share vector at v ONLY for
a final endpoint tie signature; no fallback uses the undefined ratio0/0 there.

Let Phi be an invertible homogeneous projective map with affine denominator
D(x)>0 throughout P. Each consistently oriented transformed facet slack is
c_j*slack_j(x)/D(x), c_j>0. Consequently normalized slacks transform as

    z'_j(Phi(x))=[D(p)/D(x)] z_j(x).                         (2)

The multiplier is common to all j, so every r_j and rho is unchanged. This
also absorbs arbitrary positive row scaling. Positive projective maps preserve
segments, supporting faces, ordinary edges, polygon distances and incidences.
The policy's tie signatures use ordered share vectors of endpoints and path
vertices, with fixed original row labels. Its ENTIRE route, including phase
resets and tie breaking, therefore transforms equivariantly. Arbitrary row
permutations can alter ties; no such invariance is claimed.

## 2. A decreasing edge is derived, not supplied

For h=1 the retained segment reaches v directly. Suppose h>=2 and x has no
new target equality. Choose j realizing rho(x). Simplicity at v makes
P_J intersect {slack_j=0} an(h-1)-face, so it has a vertex w!=v. Then
z_j(w)=0 and S(w)>0. The derivative numerator of z_j/S at x toward w is

    S(x) dz_j(w-x)-z_j(x) dS(w-x)=-z_j(x)S(w)<0.             (3)

The tangent cone of the simple retained face is generated by its eligible
incident original-edge rays. Decompose w-x into those rays with nonnegative
coefficients. The numerator in(3) is linear in the direction, so some ray has
negative numerator. Unless it immediately acquires a target facet, its neighbor
y has S(y)>0, and the exact linear-fractional comparison gives
r_j(y)<r_j(x)=rho(x), hence rho(y)<rho(x). This proves that a strict share
improvement exists whenever fallback is needed. It does NOT bound its amount.

## 3. Entire polygon minima and the precise policy

An acquisition-free inspected two-face F is disjoint from every missing target
facet: a nonempty supporting intersection with a compact polygon contains a
vertex. Thus S>0 on F. For x=sum_v lambda_v*v in F,

    r(x)=sum_v [lambda_v*S(v)/S(x)] r(v).

The bracketed terms are convex weights. Minimum coordinate is concave, so

    rho(x)>=sum_v [lambda_v*S(v)/S(x)]rho(v)>=min_v rho(v).   (4)

Hence a minimum over polygon vertices is the WHOLE polygon minimum. This is a
quasiconcavity argument, not a claim that rho is affine or concave in x.

Reuse #253's complete original-H polygon checker: at a simple vertex every
eligible two-face fixes d-2 original rows, and has at most e+2 corners where
e=m-d. The checker verifies inverse identities, feasibility, both boundary
neighbors at each corner, complete cycles and distinct original facet labels.
No ambient graph, LP, inverse-discovery or neighbor oracle is used by the audit.

The new rule prefers an immediate new target equality. Otherwise inspect all
binom(h,2) eligible polygons and choose a shortest first-acquisition arc when
one exists. If none exists, select the lowest rho among ALL inspected corners,
then a shortest boundary arc to an endpoint realizing that minimum. Ties use
the negative ordered share tuple at the endpoint, then those along the path.
Every candidate is globally checked on its complete ORIGINAL two-face.

Both acquisition and fallback arcs have at most

    a=floor((e+2)/2)                                       (5)

edges. An intermediate vertex may increase rho or decrease the old phase
objective; ONLY decision anchors must improve rho. No intermediate rho increase
occurred in the benchmark, so that permitted case is not falsely counted as
executed coverage. Original edge validity and target-face preservation are
checked for every intermediate step regardless of objective behavior.

## 4. Permanent charges and the bound that remains exponential

Call an inspected face improving if its minimum is below rho(x). A decreasing
incident edge lies in h-1 eligible two-faces, all improving. The chosen endpoint
z has rho no larger than every inspected minimum. Later fallback anchors in
the SAME phase have strictly smaller rho, so an already charged face cannot
improve again. It may be inspected again but cannot receive another charge.

After acquisition, the newly locked target facet is disjoint from all previously
inspected failed-acquisition faces. They cannot reappear in later retained
faces. Thus these improving-face charges are permanent across phase resets.
Each face in an h-phase is labeled by the locked rows and h-2 non-target rows;
there are at most binom(e,h-2) labels. Therefore

    B_h<=floor(binom(e,h-2)/(h-1)), h>=3.                    (6)

There is no fallback when h<=2 because the entire retained segment/polygon
contains the target. The h-values decrease after acquisitions. For r initial
missing target facets the committed route has

    L<=a*[r+sum_(h=3..r) floor(binom(e,h-2)/(h-1))].          (7)

This improves only the fallback coefficient from the previous e+1 to a. The
shorter-arc argument ALSO applies to the old linear potential if it is required
to improve only at anchors. It is NOT uniquely a benefit of shares; their
separate benefit is projective invariance. No third-policy ablation was run.

The sum in(7) is still exponential for unrestricted dimension and facet excess.
It is not Polynomial Hirsch or a new best general diameter bound. The auditor
checks fresh labels, whole-face minima, h-1 new charges, all macro caps and the
aggregate. Decision anchors cannot repeat. Intermediate vertices can in principle
repeat, so raw cost and a separately checked loop erasure are both returned.
No tested raw path needed erasure.

Each decision explores at most binom(h,2)(e+2) polygon-corner occurrences.
Rational coordinates/ratios have original-minor-controlled bit sizes. This is
polynomial work PER DECISION, not a polynomial total decision bound. Simple,
bounded, full-dimensional original-H input is a stated class, not inferred
globally from one basis. Strictly inactive redundant rows only justify a larger
row-count bound, not a falsely reduced genuine-facet count.

## 5. A fixed-type obstruction for the OLD radial gap

Let the previous objective be f and Q(x)=f(v)-f(x)=S(x), with Q(p)=h. For
0<epsilon<=1 put

    D_epsilon(x)=epsilon+(1-epsilon)Q(x)/h,
    Phi_epsilon(x)=(x-v)/D_epsilon(x).                       (8)

Target slacks are nonnegative, so D>=epsilon throughout the WHOLE P. In
target-centered coordinates the homogeneous matrix is[[I,0],[q,epsilon]],
with determinant epsilon. Thus this is a genuine positive projective map,
preserving every face and the number of genuine facets.

If alpha=1-Q(x)/h is the old relative gain, its transformed value is

    alpha'=epsilon*alpha/[1-(1-epsilon)*alpha].              (9)

For fixed0<alpha<1, this tends to zero. Q/D is strictly increasing in Q, so
the old first-phase objective ordering, polygon maxima and strict improving
arcs are preserved. Endpoint signatures at equal Q get a common positive
factor. The selected best endpoint and its gap are preserved; additional ties
between different arcs to that endpoint need not be projectively invariant
for the OLD rule. The explicit experiment checks that its arc also agrees.
No full projective invariance is claimed for that previous rule.

Use the truncated octahedron |x_i|<=2 and all signed coordinate sums<=3, with
p=(2,1,0),v=(-2,-1,0). It has3 dimensions,14 facets and no target acquisition
on a source-incident two-face. The old first fallback uses3 edges and gap
fraction7/18. With epsilon=2^-k, it becomes exactly

    7/(11*2^k+7).

The new first fallback instead uses2 edges and rho1/3 to4/25, and its entire
six-edge route is projectively unchanged. Tests cover k8,40,120,240, with
whole-set denominator positivity from(8), not vertex sampling as a premise.
This excludes a coefficient-independent positive fraction of the OLD gap at
fixed dimension/facet count, not a polynomial route bound or graph diameter.

## 6. The NEW angular score also has a fixed-type small-gain obstruction

3-dimensional polytopes with12 genuine facets and the same20-simple-vertex
face lattice for every0<delta<=1/64. The first complete-face fallback satisfies

    rho_before=1/3, rho_after=(1-13*delta)/3,
    relative decrease=13*delta.                             (10)

Thus even the invariant angular score has no coefficient-independent positive
fractional gain. This is a genuine deformation, not an asserted projective
re-encoding: its invariant gain actually changes. The first inspected4/5/6-gon
faces all miss the target facets, so the fallback cannot be bypassed by one
of the already-enumerated acquisitions.

The all-parameter argument has20 determinant certificates,60 exact active
identities and180 strict rational slack inequalities, with nonnegative
Bernstein-coefficient certificates on0<64*delta<=1. Twenty simple feasible
vertices saturate v<=2f-4 for f=12; hence no extra/nonsimple vertices are missed.
The symbolic checker separately proves the fixed angular first-star vertices
and exact coefficient13. Five complete numerical instances through delta2^-240

This is again not a slow trajectory or diameter lower bound. It closes off

## 7. A projective roof normal form: useful but not a solved premise

In a full-dimensional phase with no locked rows, use the d independent target
slacks as affine coordinates z around v. Intrinsically the same construction
works in a retained h-face with its remaining h target slacks. Positive scaling
makes the anchor all one. The other inequalities become B_i z<=beta_i with
beta_i>0. For z!=0 let u=z/sum(z),t=1/sum(z). The exact image is

    u>=0, sum(u)=1, t>=max_i B_i u/beta_i.                  (11)

Boundedness of P forces that maximum positive everywhere on the simplex;
otherwise u would give a nonzero recession direction. Compactness makes its
minimum positive, so(11) already implies t>0 and z=u/t is the inverse.
Finite vertices are original non-target vertices; finite edges are original
edges not ending at v. Target-incident edges become unbounded rays. The new
potential is min(u_j), so acquisition means reaching the simplex boundary.

This is a general reformulation, not a restricted easy terrain. Its convex
piecewise-linear roof may have a complicated subdivision. Proving polynomial
boundary-reaching paths in arbitrary such roofs requires new combinatorial
control. Writing(11) does not supply it. The angular obstruction above uses
this representation explicitly.

## 8. Structural guarantee, mixed benchmarks and reproduction

The new rule, like #253, is shortest for all pairs in products of polygons.
When direct acquisition is absent, rectangles cannot reveal an acquisition
that was not already incident in a factor; complete polygon-factor arcs give
shortest factor paths, whose lengths add. No fallback is needed. Projective
invariance extends this to all positive projective images of those products,
without a chart supplied to the constructor. This is a guarantee for the rule,
not a new classical product-distance theorem or complete product recognizer.

Fresh tests on15-gon products in dimensions4/8/12 give12/24/36 shortest edges,
also after nonaffine projective changes. The12D/90-facet case has11,390,625
vertices by its known product structure;161 polygons and875 face-edge
occurrences are explored, not a full graph.
