## 1. A quantitative route-or-cut theorem

Let R be any relation on a vertex type V. Let a finite family of regions S_i
have local padded routing budgets C_i: every pair of points in S_i can be joined
by an R-route of length at most C_i. Let there also be E explicitly listed
bidirectional surviving edges. For any endpoints u,v, either:

- there is an ambient R-route of budget `sum_i C_i + E`; or
- there is a set U containing u but not v, every supplied region preserves
  membership in U, and both endpoints of each supplied edge lie on the same
  side of U.

A region preserves U precisely when any two of its points have the same
U-membership. Empty regions are harmless. Endpoint coverage need not be assumed:
add two zero-cost singleton terminal regions. Surviving edges become two-point
regions of cost one.

If the terminal regions are connected in the region-intersection graph, erase
repeated region labels from a connecting walk. Route between successive shared
points inside each used region. Each available label is charged at most once.
If the terminals are disconnected, take U to be the union of regions in the
source component. Any region meeting U must itself lie in that component, so
no region crosses the resulting cut.

The alternatives need not be exclusive for R: an unlisted ambient edge might
cross U. A cut certifies insufficiency of the **supplied repair network**, not
disconnection of the ambient polytope.

Declarations in `Solutions/PolynomialPortalCut.lean`:

- `region_route_preserves_closed_cut`: closed cuts obstruct every route using
  only the supplied region-jump relation.
- `route_or_region_closed_cut`: finite region-only quantitative dichotomy.
- `region_connectivity_iff_no_closed_cut`: exact qualitative connectivity criterion.
- `route_or_mixed_closed_cut`: regions plus surviving bidirectional edges.
- `route_of_mixed_cut_crossing`: crossing every separating cut suffices for the
  stated quantitative route, without a pre-existing covered sequence.

For the polytope application, use `S_i = extremePoints(P) intersect F_i` for
an extreme face F_i. The earlier `extreme_face_region` theorem converts an
intrinsic face-diameter proof to the local R-routing hypothesis with R = Adj P.
One must not use all points of a face as a routing region when the diameter
hypothesis only connects its extreme vertices.

## 2. Crossing intervals alone fail by an unbounded amount

Take the cycle on 2m vertices, m >= 3, labelled 0,...,2m-1. Supply just two
unit-cost edge regions:

```
S_0 = {0,1},        S_1 = {m-1,m}.
w = [0, m-1, 1, m].
```

Record damage intervals [0,2) and [1,3). Their endpoints are certified by S_0
and S_1 respectively. They cross chronologically and together cover all three
old step indices. Consequently a requirement that only uncovered steps survive
is vacuous. Nevertheless the regions are disjoint, and the source-to-target
ambient graph distance is exactly m.

The region costs always sum to two. Even the loose proposed bound
`old_length + sum_costs = 3+2 = 5` fails once m >= 6. Thus there is no universal
constant bound depending only on this fixed old length and these two unit
repair costs. Replacing ordered disjoint blocks by arbitrarily crossing
endpoint intervals is not a valid extension of the earlier theorem.

`PolynomialPortalCutCounterexample.lean` gives the explicit six-cycle witness,
including absence of any two-step ambient route and absence of a region-only
route of **any** budget. `PolynomialCyclePortalObstruction.lean` proves the
unbounded graph family symbolically, not by sampling m. Its potential is

```
h(x) = min(x, 2m-x).
```

Every graph step increases h by at most one. Since h(0)=0 and h(m)=m, every
route from 0 to m has at least m steps. The forward half-cycle attains m.

These results do not assert that this four-point sequence is generated by a
particular projective-removal procedure. Such a procedure may impose additional
geometric constraints. The result rules out an implication based only on the
weaker endpoint/interval information.

## 3. Explicit rational polygon realizations

There is a simple two-dimensional realization of every cycle in the family.
For n=2m, take vertices p_j=(j,j^2), 0 <= j < n, with H-inequalities

```
(2k+1)x - y <= k(k+1)       for k=0,...,n-2,
y - (n-1)x <= 0.
```

At p_j the lower-row slack is `(j-k)(j-k-1)`, nonnegative for integer j, and
zero exactly at k,k+1. The upper-row slack is `j(n-1-j)`, zero exactly at 0,n-1.
Adding the first and last lower rows separately to the upper row yields
`0 <= x <= n-1`; the displayed inequalities then bound y. The first three
vertices have nonzero affine determinant 2. Thus these are bounded, full-
dimensional rational polygons with the required cyclic edges.

The exact Python checker enumerates all nonparallel row-pair intersections,
checks feasibility with rational arithmetic, checks the complete vertex set,
checks two tight vertices per row, reconstructs the graph, and verifies the
endpoint distance for n=6,12,20,50. These rational H-polygon certificates are
**not Lean hull formalizations**. The Lean result is the abstract cycle theorem;
the displayed algebra and Python checker separately support its geometric
realization.

The family does not contradict Polynomial Hirsch: n grows with m, and its
diameter m=n/2 is linear in n. It also does not invalidate the earlier ordered-
block theorem, whose ordering hypothesis explicitly fails here.

## 4. Executable certificates and exact tests

`scripts/portal_cut_certificate.py` builds a finite weighted intersection
network with explicit shared-vertex portals. It returns either a nonrepeating
region/edge chain with integer cost or a separating vertex cut. An independent
checker verifies each portal, terminal, cost, and cut-closure condition.

The solver minimizes the supplied uniform region charges. This is not a claim
to minimize the actual ambient graph distance. Establishing local diameter
budgets and facehood remains a caller obligation; the executable does not infer
those hypotheses from a list of vertices.

The regression suite compares certificates with a separately implemented vertex
closure computation on:

| Test universe | Endpoint cases |
|---|---:|
| All 2,048 families of non-singleton subsets of a four-vertex universe | 32,768 |
| All 1,024 undirected bridge graphs on five vertices | 25,600 |
| All 16 region families and 8 bridge graphs on three vertices | 1,152 |
| Total exhaustive endpoint cases | 59,520 |

Additional tests cover duplicate regions, unused expensive regions, an empty
region, zero-cost singletons, altered cost certificates, and invalid cuts.
The rational polygon witnesses also verify a positive repair: adding the
missing bridge chain gives certified cost m, exactly the true endpoint distance.

Run:

```
python3 scripts/portal_cut_certificate.py --output portal_cut_logs/regression.json
lake build Solutions.PolynomialCyclePortalObstruction \
  Solutions.Sol_Hirsch_mixed_repair_route_or_cut

lake env lean portal_cut_packet/solution.lean
```

## 5. What remains mathematically substantive

The generic network theorem does not prove that projective-removal damage
produces a connected, polynomial-cost network. That is the remaining geometric
obligation. A candidate argument should now construct actual shared parent
vertices or surviving bridges and explain why every terminal-separating cut is
crossed, while controlling the total charged region/bridge cost.

A disconnected certificate identifies the precise missing connection instead
of accepting temporal overlap as a substitute. More formal restatements of
connectivity will not close this gap. The next useful experiment is to extract
the supplied network from genuine projective-removal instances and inspect
whether the resulting cuts require new geometry or reveal a real failure of
the proposed construction.
