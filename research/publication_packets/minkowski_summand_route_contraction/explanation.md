# Actual Minkowski decomposition, not arbitrary projection

This packet constructs the canonical summand-vertex map and proves the whole exposed-face geometry needed to contract a finite walk in R=P+Q. It reverses the earlier forward Minkowski fibre-lifting direction. It does not modify that owned proof or resubmit #308's special moment-family theorem.

At an actual extreme z=p+q, a nontrivial convex decomposition of p or q would translate to one of z; hence both components are extreme. If z=x+y is a second decomposition, z is the midpoint of x+q and p+y. Extremality forces x=p and y=q. Thus the component map is DERIVED, not an input matching or an arbitrary projection.

Suppose the entire exposed sum face is the nondegenerate segment [p0+q0,p1+q1], exposed by f. Maximality forces both endpoint components to maximize the SAME f on their factors. Cross-sums therefore belong to the whole sum segment. Write D=(p1+q1)-(p0+q0). The cross-sum p1+q0 gives p1-p0=alpha*D with 0<=alpha<=1, and q1-q0=(1-alpha)*D. For any factor maximizer x in P, both x+q0 and x+q1 belong to the sum segment, at parameters s and t. Their difference yields t=s+1-alpha. The interval constraints force 0<=s<=alpha, so x is in [p0,p1], including alpha=0. Convexity supplies the reverse inclusion. Hence this is the WHOLE exposed component face, not just aligned endpoints or a subsegment. Repeat for Q.

Apply that construction at every sum-walk edge. Canonical decompositions give actual factor vertices, and each step is either stationary or a genuine exposed factor segment. The unchanged accepted schedule-compression proof removes only stationary steps, preserving endpoints and producing an original exposed/extreme-edge walk of length at most N in each factor. Simultaneous parallel changes in both factors are allowed. The theorem does not assume that one component is fixed at each edge.

All geometric proof bodies are new here. Only compress_schedule is reused verbatim from accepted #306, with a different containing namespace; its old public target is not resubmitted. The public preamble is Mathlib and options only, the sum is inlined, and five transitive axiom printouts cover decomposition, whole-face transfer, two-factor splitting, route contraction and the public root. Convexity is explicit. No compactness, finite-hull structure, simplicity or full dimension is needed.

Scope: a finite ACTUAL sum walk is input, not a conclusion of a universal diameter algorithm. The output endpoints are the canonical components of those sum endpoints. The statement does not independently prove that every chosen summand endpoint has a suitable sum-vertex lift, that an arbitrary carrier is a summand of a controlled model, or a bound on the row cost of such a model. Those are distinct applicability obligations; do not convert them into assumed solutions of Polynomial Hirsch. Arbitrary linear projection still fails: a tetrahedron edge can project to a square diagonal.

The convex-geometric phenomenon is classical, credited to Deza–Pournin, Diameter, decomposability, and Minkowski sums of polytopes, arXiv:1806.07643, Canadian Mathematical Bulletin62(4),2019,741-755, DOI10.4153/S0008439518000668. This is a formal project transfer interface, not a new historical diameter bound.
