# Active-cut injectivity on a positive convex support

## Exact formal conclusion

Write x = sum_i w_i v_i, with every w_i strictly positive, sum_i w_i = 1,
and every v_i in a convex set P. Suppose x is an extreme point of
Q = P intersect {y : C_j(y) <= b_j for every j}. Let A be the ACTUAL active
cut labels: those j for which C_j(x)=b_j.

The theorem proves two assertions.

1. If sum_i t_i=0 and C_j(sum_i t_i v_i)=0 for every j in A, then
   sum_i t_i v_i=0. Thus active-cut evaluation is injective on the affine
   direction space generated by this positive support. Coefficient uniqueness
   is not asserted when the support points are affinely dependent.
2. If the standard homogenized vectors (1,v_i) are linearly independent,
   the vectors (1,(C_j(v_i))_{j in A}) are also linearly independent.
   Therefore n <= |A|+1. The active set is the exact subtype in the statement,
   not a supplied rank witness or assumed small list.

No compactness, base-vertex enumeration, full-dimensionality, independence of
cut normals, simple-polytope hypothesis, or external support oracle is used.
Finite inactive constraints are essential to the uniform perturbation argument.
Strictly positive weights matter: arbitrary unused zero-weight points need
not be controlled by the active cuts. The n=0 hypotheses are inconsistent
(sum of an empty family cannot be one); zero ambient dimension, no cuts,
single-point supports and repeated support points cause no hidden exclusions.

## Proof

For a coefficient vector t with total zero, put z=sum_i t_i v_i. A finite
positive-margin lemma constructs epsilon>0 small enough that both
w_i+epsilon*t_i and w_i-epsilon*t_i remain positive for every i. Their sums
are both one. Convexity of P therefore places x+epsilon*z and x-epsilon*z
inside P, with the vector equalities proved by distribution of finite sums.

For a cut active at x, the assumed zero evaluation C_j(z)=0 preserves its
boundary exactly. For an inactive cut the gap b_j-C_j(x) is strictly positive.
A second use of the same finite-margin lemma makes

    epsilon*|C_j(z)| < b_j-C_j(x)

hold for EVERY inactive cut. Both perturbed points consequently lie in the
actual set Q. No inactive constraint is replaced by an equality or dropped.
The point x is their midpoint, an open convex combination. Its Mathlib
extremePoints hypothesis forces the first perturbed point equal to x, hence
z=0 because epsilon is nonzero.

For the second assertion, a zero linear combination of the homogenized active
images has first coordinate sum_i t_i=0 and remaining coordinates C_j(z)=0.
The geometric result gives z=0. Linear independence of (1,v_i) then forces
all coefficients to vanish. Finally, the ambient image space
R times (A -> R) has dimension 1+|A|, giving the cardinal bound by the standard
finite-dimensional independence theorem.

## Relation to Polynomial Hirsch and the existing cut-level work

The written #277 cut-image alphabet argument needs to know that active cuts
separate independent base directions at a new vertex. This theorem formalizes
that local mechanism directly from convexity and extremality. Given a positive
affinely independent support, it derives injectivity of its active-cut images
instead of taking that as an extra premise.

It does NOT yet select an appropriate base-vertex support, prove a full
Caratheodory/face-adapter theorem, extract a minimal independent set of active
rows, or formally enumerate and count every possible coordinate value after
cuts. Those are subsequent composition tasks. The accepted finite coordinate-
extreme route theorem is not assumed as an axiom or resubmitted here; an
unrestricted polynomial inventory for all carriers remains unproved.
This is a classical local rank fact formalized at the project's interface,
not a claim of a new classical polyhedral theorem or a solution of Hirsch.
