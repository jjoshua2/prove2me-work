# Arbitrary endpoint vertices, with objectives derived

The accepted #315 route requires two regular objectives. This packet removes those inputs. For ANY actual extreme point u of the ORIGINAL segment sum Z, derive a linear objective f which is nonzero on each nonzero generator and whose entire maximizing face is {u}. Apply the same construction independently to the requested target v, then reuse #315 unchanged to obtain at most m original exposed/extreme edges joining exactly u and v.

The finite corner set C is defined as the images of ALL Boolean coefficient vectors, not a supplied vertex list. It may contain duplicates and nonvertices. Pinned Mathlib's parallelepiped identity proves Z=convexHull(C). Because u is extreme, deleting {u} from Z leaves a convex set. Consequently u is not in the convex hull of C minus {u}. This is a finite hull, hence closed, so strict separation gives f strictly larger at u than at every other corner image. Neither simplicity nor a full-dimensional representation is used.

The canonical saturated corner V(f) maximizes f over the whole coefficient sum. Since u belongs to that sum, f(u)<=f(V(f)); strict separation forces V(f)=u. If a nonzero generator were tied, toggling its zero coefficient to one would produce a distinct corner V(f)+w_i with the same objective value, a contradiction. Thus the derived objective is regular. The accepted singleton-face theorem identifies its ENTIRE face as {u}, and the accepted route endpoints are therefore the originally requested u and v.

The entire1048-line accepted #315 namespace prefix is reused byte-for-byte, excluding only its old root and print suffix. The new proof assumes neither a regular objective nor an injective Boolean representation. The exponential finite corner set is an existence proof device, NOT a claim of polynomial-time enumeration or extraction. All zero, repeated, opposite, parallel, rank-deficient and zero-dimensional cases remain covered. The public target is explicit Mathlib notation with imports/opens/settings only and matches the top-level solution exactly.

This is the classical generator-count bound for zonotopes, now composed with arbitrary endpoint exposure. It is not a new best classical result, a shortest-path theorem, an original-facet bound for arbitrary polytopes, or unrestricted Polynomial Hirsch. In particular, m counts generators.
