## Exact mathematical result and limitations

For original finite real H-data P, an actual extreme target v and a row set J,
retain all target-tight original rows plus J to form Q. For each whole exposed
nondegenerate original segment e=[u,w] with feasible non-target endpoints, assume
its target-slack common-tight labels are all in J: the forced-row condition.
The theorem derives the exposed line section

    E(e) = Q intersect {u+t(w-u) : t in R},
    P intersect E(e) = e.

It also proves original-edge inclusion, target exclusion, injective line
parameterization, and that equal relaxed carriers imply equal original edge
sets. Thus distinct geometric edges have distinct carriers. Repeated occurrences
of the same edge are not made distinct. The supporting affine line is derived
from common-active equations and two-sided feasible perturbations, not supplied
as a rank oracle. The exposing functional is built by summing retained original rows.

Old endpoints can cease to be relaxed vertices and carriers may be longer
segments or rays. This is not a graph embedding on the old vertices. The forced
condition identifies selected edges, not a small-count or congestion assumption;
it is sufficient, not necessary when redundant rows can be deleted. Dimension
is arbitrary and no boundedness, finite-hull identity, full dimension, simplicity
or vertex catalogue is assumed. No polynomial carrier count, route construction
or Polynomial Hirsch conclusion is proved. The next geometric obligation is
quantitative control along a constructed ORIGINAL ordinary-edge route.
