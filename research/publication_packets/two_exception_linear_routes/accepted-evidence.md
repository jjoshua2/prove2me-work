m linear halfspaces. u,v are actual extreme points. B contains at most TWO original
labels. Every target-tight row outside B must take its boundary value and at most
one other value on all actual vertices; exceptional and non-target rows may have
arbitrarily many values. The constructed route satisfies L <= (m-d)+m, preserving
every target row already tight. Each edge is a whole nondegenerate IsExtreme
segment of ORIGINAL P, and every visited point is an actual extreme point.

Accepted #325 supplies a genuine good-row entry prefix of cost at most m-d and
derives that the remaining direction space has dimension at most |B|. The new
proof derives at most two actual extreme points on an affine line. In a residual
plane, every actual vertex has at least two active original rows with NONZERO
restrictions; every such row contains at most two actual residual vertices.
Double-counting yields |V| <= m. Dimension-zero/one and m=0 boundary cases give
|V| <= m+1 uniformly. The actual locked strict-ascent construction then supplies
a suffix of at most m original edges. No graph, active basis, residual rank,
vertex count or cheap finishing route is assumed.

This replaces #325's quadratic two-exception specialization by a linear bound
in the SAME original row count. #324 remains sharper with at most one exception.
Redundant rows and generators, nonsimple/lower-dimensional bodies, nontarget
exceptions, empty B, dimension zero and equal endpoints remain covered. Residual
steps may acquire no new target row. m counts displayed original inequalities;
no irredundant-facet-lattice theorem is added. There is no shortestness, all-facet
nonrevisiting, separate ambient exposure or efficient vertex-enumeration claim.

Exact H/hull equality and the at-most-two-exception/two-level conditions remain
structural hypotheses, not universally derived properties. This does NOT solve
