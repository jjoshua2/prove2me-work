# External joint circuits: support multipliers and zero-deficit rigidity

For each allocation block, a multiplier supported on that entire auxiliary block, with every auxiliary coefficient equal to one and every original-row coefficient zero, is a nonnegative null dependence. Support minimality of an external circuit therefore forces a zero auxiliary coordinate in every block. Nullness and nonnegativity give all upper support bounds; the zero coordinate makes either the anchor or one generator attain the total multiplier. This proves exact support, rather than assuming the packing coefficients are nonnegative.

At an attaining listed point, the support deficit is a sum of nonnegative weighted row gaps. It is nonnegative, and is zero precisely when every positively weighted row gap vanishes. Conversely, any shared maximizer gives the reverse inequality and hence equality. A separate elementary theorem verifies that internal-only rows have automatic nonnegative budgets at nonnegative scales; their deficit coefficients must not be labeled nonnegative.

For exact candidate support values, the zero-deficit test is the nonempty intersection of the maximizing-point sets of positively weighted original rows. This determines a coefficient's zero pattern without an original-polytope optimization. It does not determine the right-hand-side support optimum. With conservative support bounds, the criterion concerns attainment of those declared bounds, not a different exposed-face support value.
