The present theorem instead proves the missing exact active-row rank and vertex characterization, in arbitrary dimension d<m.

For every ambient x, its slack polynomial p_x has degree at most d and nonzero full-label average one. Tight original rows are distinct roots. More than d such roots would force p_x=0, giving the universal tight-cardinality bound without assuming x feasible.

Given arbitrary values on the actual tight set I, use Lagrange interpolation on those nodes to construct p of degree at most |I|-1 (or zero for empty I). Let y be its nonconstant coefficient vector and c its average over ALL m original nodes. The accepted centered evaluation identity gives row_i(y)=p(a_i)-c. Since row_i(x)=1 on I, the vector z=y+c*x has exactly the requested values there. This explicitly constructs a right inverse of active evaluation; independence or a matrix inverse is not an input. The resulting z is not required to be feasible, because this is a linear rank assertion.

An actual extreme point cannot have a nonzero direction invisible to every active row. A finite positive radius preserves each inactive strict slack in both signs, while the active rows remain tight. The two perturbed points would contradict extremality at their midpoint. Conversely, if active evaluation is injective, a positive convex combination equaling x forces both endpoints to share all active values and therefore to equal x. This proves extremality iff active evaluation is injective, for feasible x.

Finite-dimensional rank now completes the exact statement. Injectivity at an extreme point forces at least d active rows; the root bound forces at most d. If there are exactly d tight rows, the constructed surjection is square and therefore injective, giving the converse.
