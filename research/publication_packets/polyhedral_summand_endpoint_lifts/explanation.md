# Endpoint lifts without a supplied supporting objective

The previous accepted contraction theorem starts with an actual sum-vertex walk. It does not by itself ensure that arbitrary desired vertices of a summand occur as its endpoint components. This packet closes that endpoint-surjectivity gap for ANY finite original H-polyhedron P and ANY nonempty compact convex Q, including nonpolytopal Q.

At an actual extreme point u of P, every vector annihilated by the active original rows is zero. Otherwise a finite slack-margin argument would put u in an open segment between two distinct feasible points. The argument does not require boundedness, full dimension, simplicity, irredundancy, or a supplied rank witness. Summing ALL active original rows gives a linear functional f bounded above on P by f(u). Equality forces each nonnegative active slack to vanish; the active-kernel result then forces the point to be u. Thus a unique exposing functional is CONSTRUCTED from the original rows, not supplied.

Compactness and nonemptiness of Q guarantee that f attains its maximum. Its entire maximizing face is compact and has an extreme point q by the pinned Mathlib Krein-Milman lemma. Being extreme in this exposed face makes q extreme in Q. For a convex decomposition of u+q in P+Q, applying f forces both first components to attain f(u), hence equal u. Cancellation reduces the decomposition to one of q in Q, so it is trivial. Therefore u+q is an actual extreme point. This supplies compatible sum endpoints independently for ANY requested u and v.

The unchanged accepted #309 proof contracts an actual sum route. Its canonical unique decompositions identify the contracted endpoints with the requested u and v, not merely unspecified summand vertices. Stationary transitions are removed by that accepted proof, and the resulting length is at most the sum-route length. Hence any genuine uniform all-pairs sum bound B descends to P with no extra edge charge. The proof uses no assumption that the two factor endpoints share one supporting objective, and no finite factor vertex enumeration is supplied.

The public statement preserves all original inequalities. P can be unbounded or lower-dimensional; Q can be a smooth compact convex body. Compactness/nonemptiness of Q are real hypotheses. Dropping them is false in general: P=[0,1] and Q=(0,1) has sum (0,2) with no extreme points, while P has endpoints; Q=R is a closed unbounded counterexample; Q=empty also destroys all lifts. The theorem does not assert every pair of factor vertices sums to a sum vertex. Selection of a compatible q is part of its conclusion.

The route-transfer clause still ASSUMES an actual all-pairs exposed-edge route bound on P+Q. It does not construct a short-routable completion of an arbitrary P or control that completion's number of presented rows. It therefore does not settle Polynomial Hirsch. It is a concrete applicability theorem, not another conjectural decomposition.
