# Whole exposed edges of a finite segment sum

For arbitrary real vectors w_i in R^d, let Z=sum_i[0,w_i]. For a linear f write
H=sum_i max(0,f(w_i)) and F={x in Z:f(x)=H}. The intended public theorem proves
that F is a nondegenerate exposed/extreme segment with actual extreme endpoints
if and only if the generators on which f vanishes have a one-dimensional span.
The latter is expressed concretely: there is a nonzero tied w_j and each tied
w_i is a real scalar multiple of w_j. This is an exact characterization, not an
assumption that arbitrary coefficient changes are geometric edges.

## Every representation is accounted for

For x=sum_i t_i w_i with0<=t_i<=1, each term
max(0,f(w_i))-t_i*f(w_i) is nonnegative. Equality f(x)=H therefore forces t_i=1
when f(w_i)>0 and t_i=0 when f(w_i)<0. Conversely these fixed choices attain H;
only the tied coordinates are free. This holds for EVERY representation,
including nonunique coefficient representations and zero/repeated generators.
The coefficient-saturation helper printed standard-only axioms in the gate.

## Construct the whole segment, not just its direction

On ties write w_i=c_i*w_j with w_j nonzero. Freeze all non-tied coordinates by
f's signs. At the lower endpoint choose1 on negative c_i and0 otherwise; at the
upper endpoint choose1 on positive c_i and0 otherwise. Their displacement is
M*w_j, where M=sum_tied |c_i|>0. Opposite vectors contribute positively to M,
so they cannot falsely cancel the edge. Every maximizing point has displacement
a*w_j from the lower endpoint with0<=a<=M. Its parameter is a/M. Conversely
bounded convex combinations of the two coefficient vectors remain maximizing.
This supplies exact equality between F and the whole closed segment.

## Necessity and endpoint geometry

The corner b selecting all positive generators and no tied ones maximizes f.
For every tied i, so does b+w_i, obtained by changing just that coefficient.
If F=[u,v], the parameters of these two points express w_i as a multiple of
v-u. If every tied generator were zero, every maximizing representation would
equal b, contradicting nondegeneracy. A nonzero representative therefore exists;
dividing its nonzero coefficient gives the complete collinearity statement.
