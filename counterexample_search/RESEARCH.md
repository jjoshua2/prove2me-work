### Why the vertex and edge checks are complete

At a scalar-fiber vertex, a factor minimal face has dimension at most one:
a face of dimension at least two has a nonzero height-preserving tangent
and would contradict extremality. An edge interior must be nonhorizontal.
At least one factor is a vertex, or a small common height change gives a
segment through the alleged fiber vertex. Thus vertices are exactly feasible
vertex/vertex or vertex/nonhorizontal-edge combinations. This recursively
constructs the entire canonical vertex list from the completely enumerated seed.

The inequalities are invariant under all spatial sign flips. For canonical
nonnegative vertices p,q, common signed-normal row space is generated by the
common templates and e_j for every coordinate zero at both endpoints occurring
in one of those templates. The script computes that rank exactly; rank d-1
means a genuine edge. Every full edge projects to a canonical edge or a loop:
if a coordinate changes sign strictly, every common row has zero coefficient
there, so the edge must be parallel to that axis. Sign symmetry then makes
its endpoints have equal absolute coordinates. All canonical edges lift to
actual edges. Hence apex graph distance is preserved by the quotient.

Boundedness follows because each spatial coordinate occurs in both a positive
and a negative template; adding those two inequalities bounds its absolute
value. Nonnegativity of spatial template coefficients bounds t between -1 and 1.
All coefficients are finite, zero is strictly feasible, and t=+/-1 exposes
the two apices. Every row is tight at exactly one apex.

## Obstruction 1: the observed 6,11,16 amplification is only additive

If the factors have strictly height-decreasing apex paths of lengths L_i,
the fiber product has an apex path with at most

    1 + sum_i (L_i-1)

edges. Parametrize factor paths by height and sort all internal vertex events.
Between events the product of factor edges is a face, cut to a segment by the
common-height equations. At events a factor vertex pins height, giving a fiber
vertex. Coincident events only shorten the path.

The Q28 seed has the strictly decreasing six-edge height sequence

    1, 5/11, 3/7, 0, -3/7, -5/11, -1.

Projective skew preserves this order. Therefore EVERY number k of independently
height-glued copies, at EVERY positive skew, has apex distance at most 5k+1.
The (1,2,7) example attains this bound. This is a proof for the whole class,
not an inference from a finite parameter sweep.

## Obstruction 2: even arbitrary affine scalar heights of fixed seeds stay linear

Let P_i have v_i vertices and e_i edges; choose arbitrary affine heights h_i.
For a nonempty fiber Q={x_i in P_i : all h_i(x_i) equal},

    diameter(Q) <= sum_i (3*v_i+e_i-1).

Proof: if common height is forced to a constant, Q is a product of sections.
Otherwise let a be the bottom of the common height range, the maximum of the
individual minimum heights. At a vertex of Q each factor is a vertex or is
inside a nonhorizontal edge, with at least one vertex factor, by the tangent
argument above. Descend each factor from there to one of its minimum vertices
along strictly height-decreasing edges, and stop at height a. No seed vertex
can repeat. Synchronizing these paths gives at most sum_i v_i fiber edges.
At height a, a factor whose minimum is a anchors a vertex of the bottom face.

That bottom face is the product of sections R_i=P_i intersect {h_i=a}. Each
section vertex is an old vertex or a crossing of an old edge, so R_i has at
most v_i+e_i vertices and graph diameter at most v_i+e_i-1. Join two arbitrary
fiber vertices by descending each to the bottom face, moving there one factor
at a time, and reversing the second descent. This proves the bound.

For k Q28 copies (274 vertices,720 edges each), the bound is 1541k. It also
allows projective changes preserving the bounded seed face lattice. Arbitrary
scalar-height choices cannot yield a superpolynomial family from this fixed
seed. Growing-rank spatial identifications and cross-block cuts are NOT covered.
These two propositions are ordinary mathematical proofs, not Lean-checked.

## A targeted apical cut genuinely increases shortest distance

The full local evidence packet independently reconstructs the Q28 seed's
274 vertices,720 edges and652 two-faces, then uses the scalar-fiber minimal-face
classification to construct graphs for arbitrary affine height functions.
All decisive heights are compared as Fractions, then replaced by integer ranks
in their joint weak order. Explicit attaining paths are checked in rational
coordinates against the original rows. Shortest-path counts are exact integers.

One nine-dimensional 56-row spindle uses skew r=2 and

    g=(-114349,-1806403,724353,65033)/1048576,
    h=(904456,306467,-695312,670635)/1048576.

Let signed Q28 rows (a,s) be in lexicographic order. The first 28 rows are
`a.x+s*(t-g.x)<=1`; the second 28 are `a_r.y+s*(t-h.y)<=1`, where a_r=2a
for s=+1 and a otherwise. Both heights have unique seed extrema at the apices.
The base has exactly 35,990 vertices,163,546 edges, distance10, and THREE
shortest apex paths.

Write its zero-based row vectors as A_j. Set

    T=(A_35+A_41+3*A_43)/5,
    B=(4*A_0+5*A_1+2*A_3+7*A_16+4*A_28+8*A_31)/30,
    c=(64*T+B)/63.

Adding `c.z<=1` keeps the positive apex tight, the negative apex strict, and
zero strictly feasible. At the targeted vertex T.z=B.z=1, so c.z=65/63>1.
The cut preserves boundedness and the spindle XOR conditions.
